## One-paragraph project description

Soroban contracts archive when nobody extends their TTL. Extension is
permissionless by design (CAP-0046-12), which makes keeping a contract alive a
public good with no owner: every team assumes someone else is watching, or trusts
a cron job on a laptop that will eventually stop. Torch makes the responsibility
explicit. A contract or its deployer registers a watch policy on-chain — which
contract, how close to archival to act, how far to extend — and a keeper reads
that registry, checks the real TTLs over Soroban RPC, and extends before
anything archives. The registry is a contract rather than a config file because
RPC cannot enumerate a contract's ledger entries: without a declared list, no
generic keeper can know what to watch.

## How the two repos connect

`torch-contract` (this repo) is the single source of truth: the Rust registry, its
spec, and the deploy script. `torch-app` contains `packages/sdk` — a typed client
generated by hand against the same function signatures — plus the keeper that
consumes it. The app has no database of record: chain state wins. The keeper's
Postgres tables only hold extension history for display and alerting, and the
keeper can run without them.

## What is not claimed

The contract is unaudited and testnet-only. There is no payments layer: a
contract cannot read another contract's TTL, so a keeper's claim cannot be
verified on-chain, and paid claims would be trust-based. The dashboard does not
exist yet. A demo video has not been recorded.
