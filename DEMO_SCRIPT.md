# TORCH — Demo Video Script

**Length:** 75 seconds
**Format:** 1920×1080, 60fps screen capture, no face, no webcam
**Sound:** one voiceover track, light music under it, and the real click and
keyboard sounds from the capture
**Goal:** someone who has never used TORCH understands what it does, and
believes it, in under a minute and a half.

**The task:** ordering shawarma from a food delivery site. It is chosen
deliberately — it is a real errand, it needs the screen driven, and it reaches
a moment where money is about to be spent, which is where the approval prompt
earns its place.

**One rule for the edit:** every frame is a real recording of the real app.
Nothing is mocked up, no number is invented, and if a take shows TORCH getting
something wrong, re-record the take rather than cutting around it.

### 0:34–0:38 — Move the mouse

| | |

| | |
|---|---|
| **Visual** | The checkout page. TORCH **stops**. The panel goes quiet. The approval card slides in: the order, the total, and three buttons — Cancel, Edit, Approve. |

| **Note** | **Hold on the stopped state for a full three seconds before anything is clicked.** The pause is the message. Then the cursor moves to Approve and clicks it. |

## Voiceover script, clean

> This is TORCH.
>
> You tell it what you want, the way you'd tell a person.
>
> It works out the steps, and shows you all of them before it starts.
>
> Now it's using your computer. The blue border means TORCH is driving — so you
> always know when it's you and when it's not.
>
> You're not locked out. It's your computer the whole time.
>
> And before it spends your money — it stops. Every time.
>
> Then it tells you what it did, in one line.
>
> And if it gets something wrong, one button puts it back.
>
> TORCH. It runs on your computer, and it asks before anything that matters.

**Delivery:** unhurried, Nigerian accent, no announcer energy. Around 110 words
over 75 seconds is slow on purpose — the footage is doing the work.

## Before recording

- **Use a clean profile.** No personal email in the browser, no real card
  details, no other people's names in any window. The address bar, the
  notification tray and the taskbar are all on camera.
- **Use a test payment method.** If the order actually completes, use one that
  can be cancelled.
- **Blur nothing in post.** If something needs blurring, re-record instead — a
  blurred patch reads as something being hidden.
- **Check the recap is real.** Whatever line the app produces is the line that
  goes in the video, even if a written one would sound better.
- **Record the approval beat twice**, once approving and once cancelling. The
  cancel take is the one to use if the order cannot be safely completed, and
  it makes the same point.

## Known gaps

The right-hand task panel and the command pill exist but were built late; check
both render correctly at 1080p before filming. The order total shown must come
from the real site — do not overlay a number.
